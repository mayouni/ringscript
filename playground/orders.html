Route Orders — a business application that does not need the network

A sales representative walks a route of shops, takes orders from a catalogue, and the orders reach the back office later. It is one of the most ordinary applications there is, and it is built here almost entirely in Ring — the real Ring virtual machine, compiled to WebAssembly, running in this tab.

Every rule that decides whether an order is valid — the customer's price tier, the full-case discount, the tax, the stock warning, the credit limit — is written in one readable Ring file and runs on the device. The server is used exactly twice: once to pull the reference data, and again to push the finished orders. Everything between those two moments happens here.

Cut the connection below and keep working. That is the whole demonstration: take orders, price them, check credit, queue them. Nothing degrades. When the line comes back, the queue goes out — and one rejected order never loses the others.

Connection: up The server is reachable. In your product this is your own back end — Django, Laravel, Spring, Node, Go, Ring. It only ever sees JSON.

    Where the pieces live

    In Ring, on the device: the catalogue and the customer file, the price tiers, the case discount, the tax, the stock check, the credit limit, the order itself, the outbox, the sync payload, the reconciliation of the server's verdicts, and the state that is written to storage.  ·  In JavaScript: fetch, localStorage, and the DOM.  ·  On your server: two HTTP endpoints that speak JSON, in whatever language you already use.
